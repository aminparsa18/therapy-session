# Life Map: Question Bank

Purpose: when a person cannot find the origin of their pain, explore their whole life, one domain at a time, to find where it echoes. Never run this as a checklist. Choose by relevance, consent, and pace. **One question per turn.** Start with the domain closest to the presenting problem, then widen.

Before entering a heavy domain (childhood, trauma, sexuality, abuse), **ask permission**: "Would it be okay if we looked at your childhood for a bit? We can stop or skip anything." Honour a no.

For each domain, move in this order: **facts, feelings, meaning, pattern**.

---

## 1. Present snapshot (always first)
- What's the hardest thing right now, in one or two sentences?
- When did it start, or when did it get worse? What was going on in your life then?
- How are you sleeping, eating, moving, drinking/using anything?
- On a 0-10, how heavy is this today? What's the lowest it's been recently?
- What have you already tried? What helped even a little?
- What do you most want from talking today?

## 2. Childhood and early life
- What was it like in your home growing up? If you had to pick three words?
- What's one of your earliest memories? What feeling goes with it?
- Where did you feel safe as a kid? Was there anywhere you didn't?
- What were you praised for? What got you in trouble?
- What did you have to be, to be loved or at least left alone?
- Were there big events: moves, deaths, illness, divorce, money trouble, a parent away, bullying, school changes?
- Who looked after you when you were sick or scared? What did they do?
- Was there a time you felt unseen, or too much, or not enough?

## 3. Parents and caregivers (each separately)
- How would you describe your mother/primary caregiver as a person? What was she like when she was happy? Stressed? Angry?
- How did she respond when you cried or were afraid?
- How would you describe your father/second caregiver? What was your connection like?
- How did they show love? Which of those did you actually feel? What did you want and not get?
- How did they treat each other? How was conflict handled? Was anyone afraid?
- What did they teach you, directly or indirectly, about emotions, money, success, bodies, men, women, relationships?
- What were their own wounds or histories, as far as you know?
- What do you want to thank them for? What do you still carry?
- What would you want them to understand about you?
- Today: how is your relationship with each? What happens in your body before you speak to them?

## 4. Siblings and family roles
- Where are you in the family order? What role did you play: caretaker, peacemaker, rebel, achiever, invisible one, the "problem"?
- Who was favoured? Who was blamed?
- Did you grow up fast, taking care of others?
- How are you with each sibling now?

## 5. Extended family, culture, religion, community
- What did your culture or family expect of you? Where do you fit and where do you not?
- What were the unspoken family rules ("Don't cry", "Family first", "Don't talk about it")?
- What role did faith or tradition play? Supportive, harsh, complicated?
- Any migration, displacement, discrimination, or loss of home?
- Generational patterns: addiction, silence, secrecy, depression, divorce, achievement pressure.

## 6. Education and adolescence
- How was school: academically and socially?
- Did you feel you belonged? Who were your people?
- Were there bullying, comparison, pressure, shame, first heartbreak?
- What did you decide about yourself as a teenager?

## 7. Romantic relationships and intimacy
- What's your current relationship status, and how do you feel about it?
- What drew you to the partners you've had? What patterns do you notice (type, dynamics, endings)?
- When you get close to someone, what do you feel? When they pull away? When they get very close?
- How do conflicts go? Do you chase, withdraw, freeze, explode, appease?
- How were affection and sex linked to safety or shame for you?
- Have you experienced betrayal, abandonment, control, or abuse? (ask with care)
- What do you want from love that you've never fully had?
- If it's a current conflict: what is the specific hurt? What do you need? What might they need?

## 8. Friendships and social world
- Who do you turn to when things get hard? Who knows the real you?
- Do you feel lonely even among people? When did it start?
- Are your friendships balanced, or are you always giving/always taking/performing?
- Have you lost friends or communities?

## 9. Work, purpose, money
- What is your work life like? Is it aligned with who you are?
- What's the pressure: workload, boss, fear of failure, imposter feelings, burnout, unemployment?
- What did success mean in your family? What does it mean to you now?
- Money: what feelings come up? What did you learn about it growing up?
- If you could change one thing about your working life, what?
- Do you feel like your effort is seen?

## 10. Body, health, and sexuality
- How is your body doing? Any pain, illness, hormonal changes, chronic conditions?
- How do you feel about your body and appearance?
- Any history of eating struggles, self-harm, or body shame? (ask carefully, see safety.md)
- Medications or diagnoses? Last medical check?
- Where do you feel your stress or sadness physically?

## 11. Lifestyle and daily rhythm
- Walk me through a typical day. Where is there rest, joy, connection?
- Sleep: when, how long, how well?
- Movement, sunlight, food regularity.
- Alcohol, cannabis, caffeine, nicotine, other drugs, gambling, pornography, shopping, social media/gaming time: how much, why, what does it do for you?
- Screen time before bed, news and doomscrolling.
- Hobbies, play, creativity: when did you last do something just for fun?

## 12. Loss, grief, and trauma
- Who or what have you lost: people, relationships, health, home, identity, dreams?
- Was there something that felt too big or too frightening at the time? (permission first)
- Have you ever felt in danger, powerless, or violated? (do not ask for details; "you don't have to tell me what happened")
- How did you cope afterwards? Who knew?
- Is there something unspoken you've never told anyone?

## 13. Identity, values, meaning
- Who are you when no one is watching?
- Which parts of yourself do you hide, or are ashamed of? Which do you wish you could live more?
- What matters most to you: what would you stand for?
- What would you do if you weren't afraid of anyone's opinion?
- Do you feel your life is yours? Whose life are you living?
- What would you want said about you at the end?
- What feels missing: purpose, connection, creativity, spirituality, belonging?

## 14. Inner critic and self-image
- What does your inner voice say when you fail? Whose voice does it sound like?
- What would you say to a friend in the same situation?
- What do you believe, deep down, about yourself? ("I'm...")
- When did you first believe that?

## 15. Patterns, triggers, and coping
- What reliably sets you off? What do you do next? What do you get from it? What does it cost?
- When have you felt this exact feeling before? (affect bridge)
- What do you do to avoid feeling it: work, scrolling, drinking, pleasing, controlling, shutting down?
- Which coping habits helped you survive earlier but hurt you now?

## 16. Strengths, resources, and hope
- What have you survived that you're quietly proud of?
- Who or what has been good for you?
- What are you good at? What do others come to you for?
- When have you been at your best?
- What do you hope for, even a little?
- What's one small thing that would make tomorrow slightly better?

## Overthinker / obsessive-thought mini-map
When someone is stuck in a mental loop, simplify through these in order:
1. **State the question.** "What's the exact question your mind keeps asking?" (often a single one: "Will I be okay?", "Am I a bad person?", "Did I ruin it?").
2. **Fact or fear?** "What do you know for certain? What are you predicting or imagining?"
3. **What's the feeling underneath the question?** (fear, shame, grief, anger, helplessness)
4. **What does the mind believe will happen if it stops thinking about this?** (the hidden function: control, safety, preventing regret)
5. **When did you first need to be this vigilant?** (origin: unsafe, unpredictable, or critical environment)
6. **Is this solvable now?** If yes, one concrete step. If no, the work is tolerating uncertainty and feeling, not solving.
7. **What does the loop cost you? What are you missing while inside it?**

## Threads-to-theme worksheet (for the therapist's private tracking)
Keep, silently, a running grid:

| Domain | Event or pattern | Feeling | Belief about self/others | Coping response |
|---|---|---|---|---|

When a belief or feeling repeats across 3+ domains (e.g. "I must earn love by being useful"), that is your candidate **core theme**. Bring it back to the person tentatively in the formulation.
