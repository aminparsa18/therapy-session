# Modalities Reference

How to use each approach in a text conversation. These are drawn from widely practised, well-known frameworks. Evidence strength varies and is noted honestly. Use them as lenses and tools, never as dogma. Integrate.

Evidence key: **Strong** = many controlled trials; **Moderate** = some trials / broad clinical support; **Clinical tradition** = influential and widely used, weaker or mixed trial evidence.

---

## 0. Stabilisation (use first when someone is flooded)

Before any exploration, if the person is panicking, crying hard, dissociating, or can't think:
- **Slow exhale breathing:** in for 4, out for 6 to 8, five rounds. Long exhale engages the parasympathetic response.
- **5-4-3-2-1 grounding:** 5 things you see, 4 you can touch, 3 you hear, 2 you smell, 1 you taste.
- **Orienting:** look around the room slowly and name what is safe and ordinary.
- **Cold water / temperature (TIPP):** cold water on face or holding something cold. Do not suggest if they have heart conditions without caution.
- **Feet and weight:** press feet into floor, feel the chair holding you.
- Then ask: "A bit steadier? Want to continue, or stay here a moment?"

## 1. Person-centred (Carl Rogers): the foundation of everything
**Evidence:** Strong for the common factors (alliance, empathy, genuineness).
- Core conditions: **unconditional positive regard, empathy, congruence (genuineness).**
- In practice: reflect, do not direct. Trust the person's capacity to grow. Never argue with a feeling.
- The therapeutic relationship is the best predictor of outcome across all modalities. If any technique damages the felt sense of being understood, drop the technique.

## 2. Cognitive Behavioural Therapy (CBT), Aaron Beck, Albert Ellis
**Evidence:** Strong (anxiety, depression, OCD with ERP, insomnia, panic, PTSD variants).
**Use for:** rumination, catastrophising, anxiety, low mood, perfectionism.
- **ABC / situation-thought-feeling-behaviour:** separate what happened from what they told themselves.
- **Thought record:** situation, automatic thought, emotion (0-100), evidence for, evidence against, balanced thought, re-rated emotion.
- **Cognitive distortions** (name gently, don't lecture): catastrophising, mind-reading, all-or-nothing, emotional reasoning, personalising, should-statements, discounting the positive.
- **Downward arrow:** reveals core beliefs ("I'm unlovable", "I'm incompetent", "I'm not safe").
- **Behavioural experiments:** test a prediction in the real world.
- **Worry postponement:** schedule a 15-minute "worry window"; jot worries down, defer.
- **Behavioural activation:** for low mood, schedule small valued/pleasant activities *before* motivation arrives.
- **Problem vs. feeling sort:** "Is this solvable? If yes, one next step. If no, it's a feeling to be held, not a puzzle to be solved."

## 3. Acceptance and Commitment Therapy (ACT), Steven Hayes
**Evidence:** Moderate to strong (anxiety, depression, chronic pain, OCD, avoidance patterns).
**Use for:** people fighting their own thoughts and feelings; stuck in avoidance; overthinkers.
- **Cognitive defusion:** "I'm having the thought that I'm a failure" rather than "I'm a failure." Thank your mind. Sing the thought in a silly voice. Thoughts are events, not facts.
- **Acceptance / willingness:** making room for discomfort instead of struggling. "Struggle is the second arrow."
- **Present-moment contact, self-as-context** (the observing self that notices the thoughts).
- **Values clarification:** "What do you want your life to stand for? If this pain weren't in the way, what would you do?" Values are directions, not goals.
- **Committed action:** one small step toward a value, even with the discomfort.
- Metaphors: passengers on the bus, tug-of-war with a monster, quicksand.

## 4. Dialectical Behaviour Therapy (DBT) skills, Marsha Linehan
**Evidence:** Strong (emotion dysregulation, self-harm, borderline personality features).
**Use for:** intense emotion, impulsivity, reactivity, crisis moments.
- **Distress tolerance:** TIPP (Temperature, Intense exercise, Paced breathing, Paired muscle relaxation), distraction (ACCEPTS), self-soothing with senses, radical acceptance.
- **Emotion regulation:** name the emotion, check the facts, **opposite action** (act opposite to an unhelpful urge: approach what fear says avoid), PLEASE (treat physical illness, balanced eating, avoid mood-altering substances, balanced sleep, exercise).
- **Interpersonal effectiveness:** DEAR MAN (Describe, Express, Assert, Reinforce, stay Mindful, Appear confident, Negotiate), GIVE, FAST.
- **Dialectics:** hold two truths: "I'm doing my best AND I need to do better."
- **Wise mind:** the overlap of emotion mind and reason mind.

## 5. Depth / psychodynamic approaches (Freud's legacy, Winnicott, Klein, Bowlby, others)
**Evidence:** Moderate (comparable to other therapies in meta-analyses for many conditions, especially longer-term); some concepts unfalsifiable. Use as a lens for patterns.
- **Repetition:** we unconsciously recreate early relational patterns in adult life ("Why do I keep choosing the same kind of partner?").
- **Defence mechanisms:** denial, projection, intellectualisation, displacement, reaction formation, splitting, humour. Name them with compassion: they once protected the person.
- **Transference** (as a lens): feelings about a parent showing up with a partner or boss.
- **Affect bridge:** follow a current feeling back to the earliest time it was felt.
- **Winnicott:** "good enough" parenting; false self vs. true self (performing to please).
- **Unconscious meaning:** slips, dreams, and patterns as hints, never as proof.
- Caution: do not assert hidden meanings. Offer as "I wonder."

## 6. Jungian / analytical psychology (Carl Jung)
**Evidence:** Clinical tradition; limited trial evidence; rich as a framework for meaning, midlife, creativity, dreams. Treat as a metaphorical map, not science. Jung's typology (MBTI) has weak scientific validity. Do not type people.
**Use for:** identity, meaning, midlife crisis, recurring dreams, inner conflict, "I feel split", creative blocks.
Key concepts and how to use them:
- **Persona:** the mask we show the world. Ask: "Who do you have to be to be accepted? What does it cost?"
- **Shadow:** disowned parts of ourselves (anger, ambition, neediness, sexuality, but also unlived gifts). Clues: strong irritation at others' traits, envy, projections. Ask: "What do you most dislike in others? Where might a trace of that live in you?" and "What qualities do you admire intensely but feel you can't have?"
- **Anima / Animus:** inner contrasexual figures, or more broadly the inner "other" and how we relate to the unlike-us. Use gently and non-stereotypically; many modern Jungians treat them as symbolic of inner relational capacities.
- **Self and individuation:** the lifelong movement toward wholeness by integrating conscious and unconscious. Frame as "becoming who you are, not who you were told to be."
- **Complexes:** emotionally charged clusters triggered by themes (a "mother complex", "inferiority complex"). Cue: a disproportionate reaction. Ask what it echoes.
- **Archetypes:** recurring human patterns (Hero, Caregiver, Orphan, Trickster, Wise Elder). Helpful for narrative: "Which role did you play in your family?"
- **Dream work:** ask for the dream, the feeling in it, the associations *the dreamer* has to each image (amplification). Never decode with a dictionary. "What does this image mean to you?"
- **Active imagination:** dialogue with an inner figure in writing or imagery ("Let the figure speak. What does it want?").
- **Midlife transition:** the first half of life builds the ego; the second asks "what is this for?"
- Always keep it grounded: symbolic insights must connect to real-life choices.

## 7. Attachment theory (John Bowlby, Mary Ainsworth; adult attachment research)
**Evidence:** Strong as a developmental framework; adult styles are dimensions, not boxes.
**Use for:** relationship patterns, fear of abandonment, avoiding closeness, "I'm too much/not enough".
- Patterns: **secure**; **anxious** (hyper-activating: protest, pursue, need reassurance); **avoidant** (deactivating: withdraw, self-reliance, discomfort with closeness); **disorganised** (want closeness and fear it).
- Ask about early caregiving: "When you were upset as a child, what happened? Who came? What did you learn to do with your feelings?"
- Link to now: "When your partner pulls away, what does your body do? What story starts?"
- Hope: **earned security** is possible through safe relationships, reflection, and therapy. Styles are changeable.

## 8. Internal Family Systems (IFS), Richard Schwartz
**Evidence:** Emerging to moderate (promising trials; growing). Use as a compassionate lens.
**Use for:** inner conflict, self-criticism, addictive/avoidant behaviours, "parts" language.
- Model: the mind has **parts**; **Self** is the calm, curious, compassionate core. Parts: **Managers** (planning, perfectionism, critic), **Firefighters** (distraction, numbing, rage, binge), **Exiles** (young, hurt, ashamed parts they protect).
- Method: find the part ("Where do you feel it in your body?"), get curious ("What does this part want for you? What is it afraid would happen if it stopped?"), thank it, don't fight it. Ask how the person feels *toward* the part. If the answer is hostile, ask that hostile part to step back.
- No forcing access to exiles in text with potential trauma; go slow.

## 9. Schema therapy (Jeffrey Young)
**Evidence:** Moderate (personality-related and chronic patterns).
**Use for:** deep, lifelong patterns that survive ordinary CBT.
- **Early maladaptive schemas:** abandonment, mistrust/abuse, emotional deprivation, defectiveness/shame, social isolation, failure, dependence, vulnerability, enmeshment, subjugation, self-sacrifice, emotional inhibition, unrelenting standards, entitlement, insufficient self-control.
- Born from unmet **core needs** in childhood (safety, attachment, autonomy, expression, play, limits).
- **Modes:** Vulnerable Child, Angry Child, Punitive/Demanding Parent (internalised critic), Detached Protector, Healthy Adult.
- Task: strengthen the Healthy Adult, give the Vulnerable Child what was missing, quiet the Punitive Parent.

## 10. Compassion-Focused Therapy (Paul Gilbert) and self-compassion (Kristin Neff)
**Evidence:** Moderate.
**Use for:** shame, self-attack, perfectionism.
- Three components: **mindfulness, common humanity, self-kindness.**
- **"What would you say to a friend?"** Ask them to say it to themselves.
- **Compassionate letter** from the wisest, kindest version of themselves.
- Self-compassion is not self-indulgence; research links it to *more* resilience and accountability.

## 11. Narrative therapy (Michael White, David Epston)
**Evidence:** Moderate / clinical tradition.
**Use for:** a person fused with a problem-saturated identity.
- **Externalise:** "How long has *the Anxiety* been bossing you around? What does it say?"
- **Unique outcomes:** times the problem didn't win.
- **Re-authoring:** which values and abilities showed up then? Build the preferred story.
- **Thickening the story:** who would not be surprised you survived this?

## 12. Solution-focused brief therapy
**Evidence:** Moderate; useful for goal clarity.
- **Miracle question, scaling, exceptions, coping questions** ("How have you managed to keep going?"), and **preferred future**.
- Great when the person is stuck on the problem and needs direction.

## 13. Motivational Interviewing (Miller & Rollnick)
**Evidence:** Strong (change behaviours, addiction, ambivalence).
- Spirit: partnership, acceptance, compassion, evocation.
- **OARS:** Open questions, Affirmations, Reflections, Summaries.
- Explore **both sides** of ambivalence, then elicit **change talk** ("What would be the good thing about changing? What makes you think you could?").
- Rolling with resistance: never argue for change; the "righting reflex" backfires.

## 14. Existential and logotherapy (Viktor Frankl, Irvin Yalom)
**Evidence:** Moderate for meaning-centred interventions (e.g., in illness, grief).
**Use for:** emptiness, loss of meaning, life transitions, death anxiety.
- Frankl: meaning from **creative work, experience/love, and attitude toward unavoidable suffering**.
- Yalom's four givens: **death, freedom/responsibility, isolation, meaninglessness**. Many breakdowns are about one of these.
- Questions: "What makes your life worth the effort? What would you regret not having done?"

## 15. Gestalt and experiential
**Evidence:** Clinical tradition.
- **Here-and-now:** "What are you feeling as you say that?"
- **Empty chair** (written): speak to a person or a part; then answer from their chair.
- **Exaggeration/ownership:** change "it" and "you" to "I".
- Use sparingly and only with stability.

## 16. Mindfulness-based (MBSR / MBCT: Kabat-Zinn; Segal, Williams, Teasdale)
**Evidence:** Strong for relapse prevention in recurrent depression (MBCT); moderate elsewhere. **Caution:** can worsen distress in some with trauma; keep short, anchored, optional.
- Anchors: breath, sounds, body. **Labelling:** "thinking, thinking". **RAIN:** Recognise, Allow, Investigate, Nurture. **Decentring:** thoughts are mental events passing through.
- "Leaves on a stream." Keep exercises 1 to 3 minutes in text.

## 17. Somatic and body awareness
**Evidence:** Limited to moderate; interoceptive awareness is increasingly supported. Polyvagal theory is influential but scientifically contested; use its **practical tools** (breath, grounding, orienting), not its claims as fact.
- "Where do you feel it in your body? Size, shape, temperature? What does it want to do?"
- Titrate: notice for 30 seconds, then look away and orient. Not for deep trauma processing in text.

## 18. Trauma-informed care (not trauma processing)
**Evidence:** Strong for trauma-focused CBT, EMDR, prolonged exposure, delivered by trained clinicians.
- **You do not conduct trauma therapy.** Provide stabilisation, validation, and psychoeducation (the nervous system adapts to threat; symptoms are survival responses), and recommend a trauma-trained clinician (EMDR, trauma-focused CBT, CPT, PE, somatic).
- Principles: **safety, choice, collaboration, trustworthiness, empowerment.** Ask before going deeper. Let them set the pace. Don't ask for graphic details.

## 19. Family systems (Murray Bowen)
**Evidence:** Clinical tradition.
- Families are emotional systems: **roles** (peacemaker, scapegoat, golden child, parentified child), **triangles**, **differentiation of self** (staying connected without fusing or cutting off), **multigenerational transmission**.
- **Genogram in words:** grandparents, parents, who was close, who was cut off, patterns of illness, addiction, divorce.
- Helps people see: "This was a pattern long before me."

## 20. Positive psychology and basic wellbeing foundations
**Evidence:** Moderate; effects modest but real.
- **Gratitude, strengths (VIA), savouring, three good things, acts of kindness, flow, social connection.**
- Use *after* pain is acknowledged. Positivity forced on grief is invalidating.

## Lifestyle factors that move mood (evidence-supported)
Ask about them early; fix cheaply:
- **Sleep:** insomnia and poor sleep strongly feed anxiety and depression. CBT-I basics: consistent wake time, no screens/bed work, out of bed if awake >20 min.
- **Movement:** regular exercise has antidepressant and anxiolytic effects.
- **Substances:** alcohol, cannabis, caffeine, nicotine, stimulants. Ask without judgement.
- **Food and hydration; light and outdoors; social contact; screen/doomscrolling time.**
- **Medical:** thyroid, anaemia, vitamin deficiencies, hormones, medication side effects can mimic psychological problems. Suggest a medical check if symptoms are new, severe, or physical.

## Common factors (why therapy works, regardless of brand)
1. A trusting, collaborative relationship.
2. Hope and expectancy.
3. A coherent explanation for the suffering.
4. New experiences (emotional, behavioural, relational).
5. Mastery and agency.

Prioritise these over technique.
